## Panel B: the 1%

### B1. Hannah Lindqvist, 44, General Partner, a seed fund that backs consumer subscription apps

**Background:** Has backed a string of consumer subscriptions and watched several household utility apps with strong installs flatten after the first couple of months. She pattern-matches on retention curves, not demos.

**Their question:** "Of the 400 beta households, how many still add or remove an item every week, and how many people in each house are doing it?"

**Disposition:** skeptical

**30-second scan:** The pricing table, then "400 beta households", then the line that the card-required trial converts to the annual plan.

**Gets their attention:**
- Receipt scanning attacks the input cost, which is where pantry apps usually die.
- A shared household list creates real switching cost once it is populated and more than one person relies on it.

**Concerns:**
- Annual-by-default out of a card trial front-loads revenue and makes early numbers look better than the habit is. The pattern I have seen fail: a refund and chargeback wave in the first weeks, then a cliff at month twelve that nobody saw coming because nobody had to decide twice.
- Partner offers at pre-seed is a second business model. Two unproven businesses inside two founders is usually zero proven businesses.

**Needs to see to say yes:** Weekly cohort retention for the beta households over at least eight to twelve weeks, defined as a household adding or removing items, split by households with one active member versus two or more; and trial-to-paid conversion from a trial that does not silently become annual.

**Verdict:** CONDITIONAL. I take the pre-seed meeting if the beta cohorts show sustained weekly upkeep by more than one person per household, the trial converts to monthly or asks first, and partner offers are out of the deck as a revenue line.

### 5. Where the money disagrees with the market

- **Data.** B3 Claire wants the in-home waste data and B2 Daniel sees the predicted basket as the real asset. A2 Tunde, A9 Marta and A10 Omar treat sharing it as a betrayal, and inside Panel B, B5 Gemma, B6 Sofia and B10 Ingrid side with them. Follow the market. Data revenue only exists if households trust the app enough to keep the list true; spend that trust early and there is no data to sell.
- **Annual upfront.** B2 Daniel likes annual cash at pre-seed; A1 Megan, A7 Brandon and A8 Wanjiru experience the trial as a trap. Follow the market on the mechanism, the money on the goal: offer annual as a clear choice at the end of an honest trial. B1 Hannah and B6 Sofia already explained what the trap costs later.
- **Focus versus reach.** B1 Hannah and B9 Tomás want one launch market; A3 Priya, A4 Lucía, A6 Seo-yeon and A8 Wanjiru want a product that works where they live. Follow the money here. The input method only works in the US and UK today, and launching worldwide with it buys sign-ups that churn. Say "US and UK first" out loud instead of letting the rest of the world find out at the scan step.
- **Business users.** A8 Wanjiru would pay more than any household; Panel B would likely call a small-kitchen plan a distraction at pre-seed. Do not build it yet, but watch for it in the beta. See the wildcard insight.

### 6. Fault lines

- **Retail format and geography:** supermarket receipts and barcodes (A1 Megan, A2 Tunde, A5 Gerald) versus kiranas, markets, delivery apps and bulk suppliers (A3 Priya, A4 Lucía, A6 Seo-yeon, A8 Wanjiru). The product only exists for the first group.
- **Device and income:** iPhone users in the US and UK can start today; people on Android and prepaid data (A4 Lucía, A7 Brandon, A8 Wanjiru, Omar's helper) wait or squint at a web link. Dollar pricing reads as a barrier in Mexico City and as confusion in Wales.
- **Household role:** the person who carries the food admin (A1 Megan, Omar's helper Maricel) sees relief; the person asked to tap (A7 Brandon, Tunde's flatmates) sees a chore. The split follows who does the work in the house, not age.
- **Privacy:** A9 Marta and A10 Omar treat purchase data as intimate (EU privacy culture, religion and health); A7 Brandon shrugs that the store already knows. The same sentence on the website wins nobody and loses the people who would evangelise.
- **Age and household shape:** A5 Gerald, older and alone, wants reminders and someone looking in from a distance; the younger shared households (A2 Tunde, A4 Lucía) want fairness, ownership and a lower price.

### 7. Wildcard insight and power move

**Wildcard insight (A8 Wanjiru):** In a household, an expired pot of cream is a sad dinner. In a small kitchen, it is lost margin. Home caterers, small cafés and community kitchens already keep stock in exercise books and spreadsheets, already think in money, and would pay more than a family. They may be the users for whom inventory upkeep is worth the effort, which is exactly the problem B10 Ingrid says kills household pantry apps.

**Power move (Panel B):** Position Larder as a money app, not a food app. Stop selling "a shared pantry" and sell "what your household stopped throwing away this month", stated in the user's own currency. That answers B1 Hannah's "why keep paying", gives B8 Aisha the shareable artefact, makes B9 Tomás's choice (it is a consumer app), and needs no partner data, which takes B5 Gemma's and B6 Sofia's objections off the table without a fight.

### 8. Blind spots

1. **The list's accuracy is the product, and nothing in the pitch measures it.** Every YES and every MAYBE quietly assumes someone keeps the list true. Megan is that someone in her house, Maricel would be in Omar's, nobody is in Brandon's or Tunde's. The pitch counts households, scans and nudges, but never whether the list matches the fridge.
2. **The incentives point in opposite directions.** The app promises less waste, which over time means buying less and buying more precisely. Partner offers are funded by retailers and brands that want the opposite. A9 Marta saw it from the user's side and B5 Gemma from the retailer's side; read together, partner offers are not a second revenue line but a conflict with the first.
3. **The household pays, but one person gets the value.** "Shared" is the growth story; the relief lands on one admin per house, while the tiers charge by member count for people who contribute little (Brandon, Gerald's daughter, Marta's empty seats). Both YES votes, Megan and Gerald, are the people least likely to grow revenue: one already pays, one never will.

### 9. Market note

Outside the US and UK, the product needs a different front door: online orders, chat orders and loose produce instead of receipts, local currency, local recipes and Android first. In the EU, partner offers would meet GDPR consent at full strength, and users like A9 Marta would want the data question answered before they sign up, not after.
