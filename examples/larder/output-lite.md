Market: world (USD pricing, US and UK supermarkets, no single-country tie). Size: 3 + 3 (lite). Assumptions: pre-launch consumer subscription app, web live, iOS not shipped, Android later; core audience is multi-person households in the US and UK; partner offers are planned, not live.
*All personas are fictional. Their reactions are a simulation, not research data.*

## Panel A: the 99%

### A1. Tomáš Novák, 29, warehouse shift lead, Leeds
**Background:** Moved from Brno seven years ago and shares a terraced house with three housemates who argue every week about who finished the milk. Runs everything on a two-year-old budget Android with a cheap SIM. A housemate sent him the Larder link, so he is here because he was asked to be.
**Why they matter:** The flatshare is where the "shared list" pain is loudest and where money is tightest.

- **First 10 seconds:** "Opens in browser, ok. Then it wants card for trial, and free one is only me. Only me is not shared, no?"
- **Works for them:** Barcode scan in the browser works on my phone, good. Shopping list from what is running low, this can end the milk war.
- **Doesn't work:** Free plan is 1 member, so one of us pays for four and then chases everybody for a pound each, and the house WhatsApp becomes a fight again. No Android app, the browser logs me out and I lose the scan halfway on the bus, so I stop scanning.
- **Wish:** A house plan where the cost splits inside the app.
- **Would they use it?** MAYBE. Flips if housemates can join for free while one person pays, or if the bill splits automatically.

### A2. Denise Carter, 44, ER nurse and mother of three, Columbus, Ohio
**Background:** Works 12-hour shifts, shops once a week, keeps the list on a fridge whiteboard her kids ignore. She has paid for annual subscriptions she forgot to cancel and now reads every trial's fine print before typing a card number. iPhone, like the rest of the family.
**Why they matter:** Dead-centre target customer and the one most likely to pay for Family+.

- **First 10 seconds:** "Okay, a nudge the day before with recipes, that is the thing I need, I throw out spinach every single week. Then I see 'card required, converts to annual' and my hand comes off the mouse."
- **Works for them:** The day-before nudge with three recipes would actually change what I cook on a Thursday. Scanning the receipt beats typing forty items after a shift.
- **Doesn't work:** If I'm on nights the week the trial ends, that's $47.88 gone before I notice, and that's my grocery top-up. No iPhone app yet, and nobody in my house opens a website for a grocery list; if my 16-year-old can't add "we're out of cereal" from her phone, the list is dead in a week.
- **Wish:** One button for the kids that adds an item without logging in to anything.
- **Would they use it?** MAYBE. Flips with a trial that needs no card, or monthly by default with a reminder before the charge, plus the iPhone app.

### A3. Rafael Mendes, 68, retired bus mechanic, São Paulo
**Background:** Lives alone since his wife died. Buys vegetables at the Wednesday feira and the rest at the corner mercado, pays cash, rarely keeps a receipt. His nephew put the link on his old Motorola with prepaid data. He thinks food apps are for people who do not open their own fridge.
**Why they matter:** The outsider and the category skeptic; he shows whether Larder is a habit or a gadget once you take away the US or UK receipt and the full house.

- **First 10 seconds:** "Muito bonito. It asks me to scan the receipt. The feira has no receipt, the tomato has no barcode. So I type? Then I am faster with the fridge door."
- **Works for them:** The reminder before something goes bad is honest, I waste less than before, mas ainda. A free plan for one person is fair to me.
- **Doesn't work:** The page is heavy for my data, I wait, I close it, and I do not come back. The partner offers: "They sell what I eat to the supermarket and call it a discount. Não, obrigado."
- **Wish:** Say "two kilos of tomato" out loud and it remembers.
- **Would they use it?** NO. Nothing in this version flips it; a quick voice or tap add for loose food might make it a MAYBE.

## Panel B: the 1%

### B1. Ines Halvorsen, 39, Partner, a pre-seed fund that backs consumer subscription apps
**Background:** Has looked at many grocery, pantry and meal-planning apps and watched early love fade when the novelty of scanning wears off. She backs habit loops, not feature lists.
**Their question:** "Of the 400 beta households, how many still have two or more members adding items in week 8, and how many of them paid?"
**Disposition:** Skeptical.

- **30-second scan:** The pricing table, then the "partner offers" paragraph, then "two founders, 400 beta households."
- **Gets their attention:** The expiry nudge with recipes is a real daily trigger, which is rare in this category. A household with several active members creates switching cost that a solo list never does.
- **Concerns:** Pantry apps die on data entry: the list drifts from reality within weeks, users stop trusting it, and churn follows. That is the failure pattern she has seen most. Two revenue models at pre-seed, subscriptions and data-funded offers, with proof of neither, and the second can poison trust in the first.
- **Needs to see to say yes:** Beta cohort retention at weeks 4, 8 and 12, split into single-member and multi-member households; trial-to-paid conversion measured on a trial without a card, so forgotten cancellations do not inflate it; one native app live with the same retention curve.
- **Verdict:** CONDITIONAL. Would lead or join the round if multi-member retention holds and the plan commits to one revenue model through the next raise.

### B2. Callum Pryce, 52, Head of Loyalty and Partnerships, a national UK supermarket chain
**Background:** Runs the chain's loyalty data and supplier-funded promotions. He already owns first-party basket data for his own shoppers through the loyalty card, and data start-ups pitch him regularly.
**Their question:** "What do you know that my loyalty card doesn't, and can I legally use it?"
**Disposition:** Interested.

- **30-second scan:** "Receipt scanning works for most US and UK supermarkets," which means they read his receipts and his competitors' too.
- **Gets their attention:** A cross-retailer view of what is actually in the cupboard, not only what was bought from him, plus a waste signal. The day before expiry is the best moment he has seen to place an offer.
- **Concerns:** Aggregated insights from a few hundred households are anecdotes, not data; he has bought dashboards from data start-ups that nobody opened twice. If consent is shaky his legal team will not touch it, and if it ever runs in the press as "app sells your fridge," his brand is in the headline next to theirs.
- **Needs to see to say yes:** Documented opt-in consent per user, written aggregation rules with a minimum group size, enough consenting households in his catchment to be meaningful, and a pilot proposal tied to one measurable outcome, such as redemption of offers on soon-to-expire categories.
- **Verdict:** CONDITIONAL. Takes a pilot meeting once there is a consented UK user base; no data purchase and no paid offers before then.

### B3. Leah Marchetti, 47, General Counsel, a consumer subscription app company operating in the US and EU
**Background:** Has handled regulator correspondence on auto-renewal and a data-sharing complaint at a previous company. Reads the pricing page before the product page.
**Their question:** "Who consented to the data that leaves the building, and can a user cancel the trial in one tap?"
**Disposition:** Hostile.

- **30-second scan:** "The 14-day trial needs a card and converts to the annual plan," then "funded by sharing anonymised, aggregated shopping insights."
- **Gets their attention:** The core value stands without either risk, so both are fixable. Being web-first means no app store payment rules yet, which keeps pricing options open.
- **Concerns:** A card-required trial that converts by default to annual is the pattern that subscription and auto-renewal rules in the US, UK and EU are written for: clear disclosure, reminders, easy cancellation; it also brings refund demands and chargebacks. Receipt data is household data: one member scans, but the basket shows other members' purchases, including medication, pregnancy tests or alcohol, which can support sensitive inferences under GDPR and UK GDPR; "anonymised, aggregated" claims get tested hard, and under CCPA sharing data in exchange for discounts may count as a sale or share that needs an opt-out.
- **Needs to see to say yes:** A trial without a card, or monthly by default with a reminder before the first charge and in-app one-tap cancel; a data protection impact assessment for partner offers; per-member opt-in with sensitive categories dropped at ingestion; documented aggregation thresholds; a privacy notice that names partner categories.
- **Verdict:** PASS on the current design. Would sign off a launch with the trial fixed and partner offers removed from launch scope.

## Synthesis

### 1. Scoreboard
Panel A: 0 YES / 2 MAYBE / 1 NO. Panel B: 0 FUND / 2 CONDITIONAL / 1 PASS.

The room is cool, not cold. Four of six (A2, A3, B1, B2) singled out the day-before expiry nudge with recipes as the reason to care, and nobody attacked the idea itself. The damage is self-inflicted and happens before the product gets a chance: the card-required trial that converts to annual stopped A2 and turned B3 hostile, the one-member free plan contradicted the word "shared" for A1, and partner offers gave A3 a reason to say no and B3 a reason to refuse sign-off. B1 is the swing vote and is waiting on one thing only: whether more than one person per household keeps using it.

### 2. High-impact changes, ranked 1 to 5

1. **Trial without a card, or monthly by default with a reminder before the first charge and one-tap cancel.**
   Fixes it for: A2 (burned by forgotten annual renewals), A1 (will not put a card down on behalf of a house), B3 (removes the auto-renewal exposure), B1 (gets a conversion number she can trust).
   **Effort:** low. **Impact:** high; unblocks the core household and the legal sign-off in one change.

2. **Make joining a household free: one member pays, everyone else joins from a link in the browser, with an optional in-app cost split.**
   Fixes it for: A1 (no chasing housemates for money), A2 (her kids can add items without an account fight), B1 (multi-member activity is the retention signal she is asking for), B2 (more active members means a fuller, more useful pantry view).
   **Effort:** low to medium. **Impact:** high; turns "shared" from a paid feature into the growth loop.

3. **Take partner offers out of launch scope and rebuild them later as per-member opt-in, sensitive categories excluded, aggregation rules published.**
   Fixes it for: B3 (consent and sensitive-data risk), B2 (clean consent is the only data his legal team will touch), A3 ("they sell what I eat"), B1 (one revenue model to prove before the raise).
   **Effort:** medium. **Impact:** high; removes the single biggest trust risk and makes the later data business actually sellable.

4. **Quick add without a barcode or receipt: voice, a "usual items" tap list, loose produce, and receipts the scanner does not support.**
   Fixes it for: A3 (feira and market food), A1 (scans that fail on the bus), A2 (thirty seconds after a shift instead of a full scan), B1 (data entry is the failure point of the category).
   **Effort:** medium. **Impact:** medium to high; it is what keeps the list true after week 3.

5. **Ship one native app first, and make the web app light and session-stable on slow connections in the meantime.**
   Fixes it for: A2 (iPhone household), A1 (Android, logged out mid-scan), A3 (heavy page on prepaid data), B1 (wants retention measured on a native app).
   **Effort:** high. **Impact:** medium to high; necessary for everyday habit, but changes 1 to 3 cost less and unlock more doors first.
